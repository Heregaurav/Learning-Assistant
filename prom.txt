4. Most Important Requirement: Video Completion Tracking
This is the most important part of the feature.
The system must track how much of each lecture a student has watched and display that progress to the teacher.
A. Teacher-Defined Completion Percentage
When creating a lecture, the teacher can set a completion threshold between 1% and 100%.
For example:
100% threshold: The student must watch the entire video to mark it complete.
90% threshold: The student must watch at least 90% of the video.
75% threshold: The student must watch at least 75% of the video.
The selected threshold determines when a lecture is considered completed.
Do not confuse the teacher-defined completion threshold with the student's actual watch percentage. These are two different values and must be stored or calculated separately.

C. Calculating Actual Video Progress
Where reliable video duration and playback information are available, calculate progress as follows:
Actual watch percentage = (Verified watched duration / Total video duration) × 100
For example:
30 minutes watched / 60 minutes total × 100 = 50%.
The system should record the student's progress for each lecture separately and preserve it across page refreshes, logouts, and subsequent visits.
Do not calculate progress merely from the number of times a student opens a link or clicks a completion button.
D. Reliable Video Tracking and Google Drive Compatibility
Important technical limitation: A regular Google Drive share link does not automatically give a website access to the video's playback position, duration, or watch events.
Before implementing tracking, inspect the available video sources and their integration capabilities.
Implement the most reliable solution compatible with the existing project:
Prefer an embeddable video player that exposes playback time and duration through a supported API.
If Google Drive playback events are accessible through a supported integration, use them to record progress.
If direct Google Drive tracking is not possible, provide a compatible alternative, such as an authorized embedded player or an approved video-hosting integration.
If a video can only be opened as an ordinary external link, do not falsely report its viewing percentage as verified. Clearly indicate that automatic tracking is unavailable for that source.
Do not claim that the system can reliably prove a student watched a video merely because the video page was opened.
Where playback tracking is supported, record progress periodically and when the player reports relevant events, such as pausing, seeking, or finishing. Handle seeking and repeated playback carefully so that jumping directly to the end does not incorrectly count all skipped content as watched.
Use a suitable progress-tracking approach that accounts for playback position and previously watched portions. Explain any limitations of the chosen method.
The goal is to provide meaningful progress information rather than a misleading completion percentage.

## 12. Additional Requirement: Embedded Video Player and Multiple Video Platforms

This requirement is an important extension to the Lecture Videos feature. Implement it alongside all previously specified requirements.

### A. Support Multiple Video Platforms

On the teacher's side, when adding a lecture, the teacher should be able to paste a video URL from supported platforms, including:

- YouTube public videos
- Google Drive videos with the necessary sharing permissions
- Other platforms that provide a supported embeddable video player
- Direct video URLs, such as supported MP4 video links, where compatible with the existing architecture

The teacher should not be restricted to YouTube alone.

The system should identify the video platform from the URL and select the appropriate embedded player when possible.

Provide a clear validation message if the URL is invalid, the video is private, embedding is disabled, or the platform is unsupported.

### B. The Video Must Play Inside Our Website

**This is a strict UI requirement: students must not be redirected to YouTube, Google Drive, or another external website just to watch a supported lecture.**

When a student clicks a lecture card, open the lecture inside the Learning Assistant application itself.

The video should play in an embedded video player within the existing website, preferably on a dedicated lecture-view page or an appropriate in-page player, depending on the current project architecture.

Examples:

- If a teacher provides a YouTube URL, convert it to the appropriate YouTube embed URL and display the embedded player.
- If a teacher provides a Google Drive URL, convert it to a supported preview/embed URL when possible and display the video inside the application.
- If a teacher provides a supported direct video URL, play it using an HTML5 video player.
- For other supported platforms, use their official embed mechanism or player API.

Do not simply save the original URL and redirect the student to it when the lecture is opened.

The lecture title, description, playback controls, and progress indicator should remain part of the Learning Assistant experience.

**Important limitation:** Embedding is not possible for every URL. Some providers disable embedding, require authentication, restrict playback by domain, or impose other restrictions. Do not bypass these restrictions or pretend an unsupported video can be embedded. Show a clear, helpful message when a video cannot be played within the application.

Do not expose a raw video URL in the student's lecture list. The URL may be stored and used internally, but the student-facing interface should display the lecture title, description, duration, and relevant progress information instead.

### D. Embedded Video Player and Playback Controls

The lecture player should provide the controls supported by the selected platform.

Where technically supported, students should be able to:

- Play and pause the video.
- Seek forward and backward using the timeline.
- Skip backward by a short interval, such as 10 seconds.
- Skip forward by a short interval, such as 10 seconds.
- Adjust the playback volume.
- Switch to fullscreen.
- Change playback speed.

Provide these playback-speed options where supported:

- 0.25×
- 0.5×
- 0.75×
- 1×
- 1.25×
- 1.5×
- 1.75×
- 2×

The default playback speed should be 1×.

Use the official embedded player controls or player APIs when necessary. Do not build fake controls that appear functional but cannot control the actual video.

For direct video files, use the native HTML5 video element or a compatible existing player component. For YouTube and other supported platforms, use their official embedded player APIs where appropriate.

Some embedded players do not expose all playback controls or allow custom controls. In those cases, use the provider's supported controls rather than promising unavailable functionality.

For example, if a Google Drive preview does not expose playback-speed control through a supported API, do not display a custom speed selector that cannot change the playback rate.

### E. Video Progress Tracking Must Continue Working

The embedded player must integrate with the previously specified lecture-progress system.

Where the selected player exposes reliable playback events, use them to record and persist each student's progress.

Requirements:

1. Save progress periodically while the video is playing.
2. Save progress when the student pauses or leaves the lecture, where possible.
3. Restore the student's previous position when they return, if the player supports seeking and restoration.
4. Calculate actual watch progress using the total video duration and reliable playback information.
5. Prevent students from receiving full completion credit simply by opening the lecture or seeking directly to the end.
6. Respect the teacher-defined completion threshold.
7. Update the student's progress display and the teacher's Analytics tab using persisted data.
8. Keep progress separate for each student and each lecture.
9. Handle player errors, unsupported sources, and unavailable durations without inventing completion percentages.

For platforms that do not expose sufficient playback information, clearly indicate that automatic tracking is unavailable or limited.

Do not compromise the existing authentication, database, or classroom authorization system to implement playback.

### F. Teacher Experience When Adding a Video

When a teacher pastes a video link, provide a simple form containing:

- Lecture title
- Description
- Video URL
- Completion threshold
- Duration, populated automatically where supported or entered manually when necessary
- Save/Publish action

Where feasible, display a preview of the detected video before the teacher publishes it.

Validate the URL and determine whether the platform supports embedding and progress tracking. Clearly distinguish between:

- Embedded playback with automatic progress tracking
- Embedded playback with limited or unavailable tracking
- Unsupported playback that cannot be embedded

Do not claim automatic tracking is available unless the integration actually supports it.
